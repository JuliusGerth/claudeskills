# Copy That Converts

Beautiful pages with weak words do not convert. Most copy fails the same three ways: it talks about the product instead of the reader, it is clever instead of clear, and it buries the action. This skill is the antidote, applied in order.

## Step 0 — The brief (never skip)

Three answers before any writing:

1. **Wie** — who exactly reads this, and what problem are they feeling? ("iedereen" = start over)
2. **Wat** — the ONE action this page exists to cause (buy, sign up, book a call). One page, one action.
3. **Waarom jij** — the strongest true reason to choose this over doing nothing or a competitor.

Everything on the page either supports the action or gets cut.

## Headline

The headline carries most of the conversion weight. Rules:

- Lead with the clearest benefit, not the cleverest pun. Clarity beats creativity every time they conflict.
- The reader must know within 5 seconds: what is this, for whom, what do I gain?
- Proven shapes to draft from (write 5+, pick the sharpest):
  - Outcome without pain: "Professionele facturen in 30 seconden, zonder boekhoudsoftware"
  - Number/proof: "3.200 tuinders bestellen hun zaden hier"
  - Question they feel: "Nog steeds elke maandag uren kwijt aan planning?"
  - Enemy: "Stop met betalen voor features die je nooit gebruikt"
- Subhead expands with the HOW/mechanism in one sentence — not a slogan repeat.

## Body — benefits before features

Feature = what it is. Benefit = what the reader's life looks like with it. Convert every feature with the "en dus…" test: "256-bit encryptie" → "en dus… niemand leest je klantdata, ook wij niet." If "en dus" produces nothing the reader cares about, cut the feature from the page (keep it for a specs section).

Order: problem the reader recognizes → your solution → proof → action. The reader should nod twice before being asked anything.

## CTA

- Verb-led and specific about what happens next: "Plan een gratis proefles" beats "Verzenden" beats "Meer info".
- Repeat the CTA after every major section on long pages; no one scrolls back up.
- Reduce fear at the button, not just desire: "Gratis, geen creditcard nodig" under the CTA removes the last hesitation.

## Proof & objection handling

- Social proof near the CTA: concrete numbers ("4,8★ uit 312 reviews") and specific quotes beat vague ones ("Geweldig!" says nothing; "Bespaart me 3 uur per week" sells).
- List the top 3 reasons someone hesitates (prijs, moeite, vertrouwen) and answer each on the page — FAQ, guarantee, "opzeggen wanneer je wilt". Unanswered objections do not disappear; they leave.
- Never fabricate testimonials, numbers, or scarcity. Fake urgency reads as fake and poisons trust permanently.

## Style pass (do this LAST, on everything)

- Sentences ≤ 15 words on average. One idea per sentence.
- "Je/jij" (or "you") more often than the product name — count both; reader must win.
- Cut filler: "innovatief", "uniek", "de beste" claim nothing. Replace with specifics.
- Read it aloud. Anywhere you stumble or would never SAY it to a customer, rewrite it.
- Scannability: someone reading only headline, subheads, and CTA must still get the full pitch.

## Deliver

Provide the copy in page order (headline → subhead → sections → CTA's), plus 2–3 headline alternatives with a one-line rationale each. Match the language (NL/EN) and formality to the audience from Step 0 — Dutch audiences generally distrust superlatives more than American ones; tone it accordingly.
